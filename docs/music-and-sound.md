# Smyana / Смяна: music and sound plan

Status: proposal, 2026-11-10. Nothing here is decided yet; it is a starting point to react to.

## The short answer

Traditional Bulgarian music is not all fast. The fast, uneven dance rhythms (7/8, 11/8 and so on) belong mostly to Thrace, Shopluk and the dance repertoire. The **Rhodopes**, where the game is set, are famous for the opposite: slow, drawn-out songs in free rhythm, sung over the deep drone of the **kaba gaida**, the large low bagpipe of the region. That is almost exactly the sound an atmospheric, slow game wants. So the plan is not to slow down Bulgarian music, it is to use the slow part of it that already exists, from the right mountains.

Proposed direction in one line: **Rhodope voices and drones inside a small, warm orchestra**, with the forest itself carrying most of the play time and music arriving at moments that matter.

## What Bulgarian music offers a slow game

| Element | What it is | How it can serve Smyana |
|---|---|---|
| Slow Rhodope songs ("бавни песни") | Long, ornamented, free-rhythm solo songs. Often about parting, longing, haiduks, the mountains. | Main melodic language. A lone voice across a valley is the sound of the game. |
| Kaba gaida | Large goatskin bagpipe with a low drone, tuned lower and played slower than the Thracian gaida. Iconic of the Rhodopes. | Drone bed under the score; one pipe at dusk by the fire; the "100 kaba gaidi" mass sound for the homecoming. |
| Kaval | End-blown shepherd's flute, very breathy and soft, with a low "kaba" register. | The forest's voice. Ideal for exploration cues and quiet moments. |
| Gadulka | Pear-shaped bowed fiddle with sympathetic strings, hoarse and intimate. | Melancholy solo lines; homesickness at night. |
| Tambura | Long-necked plucked lute. | Gentle pulse for travel cues without becoming a dance. |
| Tapan | Big two-headed drum. | Danger and combat only (Ottomans, bears). Its arrival should feel like a warning. |
| Diaphonic / drone singing | Two-part singing in close seconds over a held drone (Pirin, Shop region). | Eerie, ancient texture for the Roman ruins and deep woods. |
| Women's choir ("Le Mystère des Voix Bulgares" sound) | Open-throated, bright, harmonically strange choral writing, developed by Philip Koutev and others in the 20th century. | The village and the women left behind. Opening and homecoming. |
| Orthodox chant | Low male chant, slow, modal. | Sparing use: a chapel in the village, a burial in the forest. |
| Laments ("оплаквания") | Half-sung, half-spoken grieving. | When one of the twenty men dies. |
| Songs of men leaving for work ("гурбетчийски" / "печалбарски" songs) | A whole song tradition about men going away to earn, and the women waiting. | This is the game's own subject. Ideal source for the main theme's text and mood. |

Useful reference listening, to check the claim that the music can be slow:

- **"Излел е Дельо хайдутин" (Izlel e Delyo Haydutin), Valya Balkanska** with two kaba gaidi. A Rhodope song about a haiduk standing against the Ottomans, chosen for the Voyager Golden Record in 1977. It is the single best reference for the voice-and-drone sound.
- The Rhodope "100 kaba gaidi" ensemble recordings, for the massed drone.
- "Le Mystère des Voix Bulgares" (vol. 1) for choir.
- Any Rhodope kaval solo, for the forest sound.

## Music direction

Model: the Outcast idea Dominic described, a wide orchestral opening that sells the scale of the world, but built from Bulgarian material rather than a generic Hollywood orchestra. A small string section, low brass, kaval, gaida and voices can sound big if the writing is broad and the recording has air.

Three rules:

1. **Mostly the forest, sometimes music.** For an hour-long game with long travel, wall-to-wall music becomes noise. Most of play time is ambience (wind, birds, leaves, rain) with music fading in for discoveries, dusk, danger and loss. Games like Red Dead Redemption 2 and Ancestors work this way.
2. **The season shifts the palette.** The title is "Shift", so the score shifts too:
   - *Summer*: kaval, tambura, light voices, open major-ish modes. Hopeful, a little proud.
   - *Autumn*: gadulka and low voice come forward; the drone grows; longer pauses.
   - *Winter*: almost nothing. Wind, a single voice, low strings, silence. The men are far from home.
   - *Spring*: the main theme returns with the women's choir as they get closer to home.
3. **One theme, many forms.** A single original main melody, written in the style of a slow Rhodope song about leaving, heard as: full orchestra and choir in the intro, a lone kaval in the forest, a hummed version by a man at the fire, a lament when someone dies, and the full choir and massed gaidi at the homecoming.

### Music cue list (about one hour of play)

Roughly 30 to 40 minutes of original music is enough for a one-hour game if it is layered and reused well.

| Cue | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Opening cinematic | 2–3 min | The big one. Wide aerial over the Rhodopes, down into the forest, to the village. Stretch goal: a real folk song introduces the village (see "The intro folk song" below). Fallback: the original main theme, starting with a single voice or kaval, then strings, choir and gaida drone open out. |
| Departure from the village | 1–2 min | Women's choir, a song of parting. Ties to the gurbet songs. |
| Exploration layers × 4 seasons | 3–4 min each, loopable | Built in stems (drone / melody / pulse) so the game can thin or thicken them. |
| Dusk and campfire | 2–3 min | Gadulka or a hummed voice. Could be sung by the men in-world. |
| Discovery: Roman ruins | 1–2 min | Diaphonic voices and drone, something older than the Bulgarian material. |
| Danger stingers | several short | Tapan hits, low gaida, for wolves and bears closing in. |
| Ottoman chase through the pass | 2–3 min | The only fast, rhythmic cue: tapan and gaida in a driving uneven meter. This is where real Bulgarian dance rhythm belongs. |
| Combat loop | 2 min, loopable | Lighter version of the chase material. |
| Death of one of the men | 1 min | Short lament. |
| Winter: deepest point | 2–3 min | Near silence, one voice. |
| Return and homecoming | 3–4 min | Main theme in full: choir, massed kaba gaidi, orchestra. The emotional peak. |
| Ending cutscene (village upgraded) | 2–3 min | Warm resolution; can lead into credits. |
| Pause / menu | 1–2 min, loopable | Quiet version of the main theme. |

## Music that follows the game

**Direction (Dominic, 2026-11-10):** the music changes with the game. It shifts with season and
time of day, and when a battle or a danger comes it changes to match, tenser or faster, and
"when it's over, the music ends as fast as it began". The whole pace of a fight with bandits or
Ottomans, or of hiding the men and carts, is carried by the music. (D-036.)

How that could work. **Proposal** by Claude; a first sketch of it can be heard in
`archive/audio/2026-11-10/07-music-spike-danger.ogg`.

**Two things change at once.** *Which* music plays depends on what is happening (the state).
*How much* of it plays depends on how bad it is (the intensity).

| State | When | What plays |
|---|---|---|
| Calm | Travel, camp, nothing near | The season's piece, thin and far off, with long silences. Often no music at all. |
| Wary | Tracks, a howl, smoke on a ridge | The tune stops. Drone only, a little lower and darker. |
| Hiding | Enemies near and the men unseen | A heartbeat on the drum, two low notes a half step apart, a thin high whine. No tune. |
| Fight | The men are found, or attack | Tapan and gaida in a fast plain 2/4, the drone at full buzz. |
| After | The last enemy falls or leaves | One last blow and silence: echo and wind. Later, a lone kaval finishes the tune. |

**Rules for the changes**

1. **Into danger: at once.** The calm tune is cut off mid-phrase, on a single drum stroke. It is
   never faded. The broken phrase is the signal.
2. **Out of danger: just as fast.** The fight music stops on a blow, in time with the last enemy
   falling where that can be done. No winding down, no victory tune. The silence afterwards is
   the reward, and the unfinished kaval phrase comes back only after ten seconds or so.
3. **Intensity is a dial, not a switch.** While hiding, the heartbeat quickens and the whine
   grows as enemies come closer, and slackens as they move off. In a fight, more enemies or
   wounded men add layers (a second drum pattern, a second gaida) rather than a new piece.
4. **Hiding to fight and back are both possible.** Slip away mid-fight and it drops to the
   heartbeat again; get found and it leaps.
5. **Who the enemy is changes the colour.** Wolves and bears: drum and drone only, no tune,
   because animals have no music. Bandits: the gaida fight tune. Ottomans: their own sound
   (a zurna, a shrill reed pipe, and their own drum), so the player hears who is coming before
   seeing them.
6. **Season and hour set the calm piece** (the palette in rule 2 of "Music direction"), and
   night makes everything thinner and lower.

**What the game needs for this.** Dominic, later on 2026-11-10: "We would swap tracks, not
record one that mixes. So wed be playing calm, then sudden swap to fight music and them back
again potentially" (D-042). So each state is its own track, and the game cuts between whole
tracks. The first idea here, layers faded in and out by an intensity dial (rule 3), is set
aside unless swapping proves too blunt. Danger tracks start on their first sound and loop;
a single drum blow covers the cut when danger ends. Creel has no music system yet; that will
need an issue. The full list of tracks is in [`music-tracks.md`](music-tracks.md).

## The intro folk song (stretch goal)

**Decided (Dominic, 2026-11-10):** the intro, which introduces the village, uses a real Bulgarian folk song, ideally one we record or produce ourselves. This is a stretch goal. Everything after the intro is original music.

**Fallback if the stretch goal isn't met:** the intro uses the original main theme instead, opened by a solo voice (or kaval) singing it in folk style. The theme is written to work as a song from the start, so swapping it in later costs nothing, and the cinematic is cut to the same length either way.

### What is free to use

- **The traditional melody and words of a folk song are public domain.** Nobody owns "Izlel e Delyo Haydutin" as a song. We can sing, arrange and record it ourselves.
- **Existing recordings are not free.** Valya Balkanska's recording, Le Mystère des Voix Bulgares albums and radio archive recordings are all under copyright and would need a licence.
- **Named arrangements are not free either.** Many famous versions are 20th-century choral arrangements by composers such as Philip Koutev or Krasimir Kyurkchiyski, and those arrangements are still under copyright. Our arrangement must be our own, built from the plain melody, not transcribed from one of theirs.
- **Old published collections are free to use as sources.** The Miladinov brothers' *Bulgarian Folk Songs* (1861, texts only) is public domain and scanned online. Vasil Stoin's collections from the early 20th century (he died in 1938, so they are out of copyright in the EU) include melodies; the exact editions need checking. Later scholarly collections, like Kaufmann and Todorov's *Folksongs from the Rhodope Region* (1970), are great for research but still under copyright, so use them to learn, not to copy from.

### Shortlist for the intro

| Song | Why it fits | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Излел е Дельо хайдутин** (Izlel e Delyo Haydutin) | Rhodope song, slow and free in rhythm, about a haiduk standing against the Ottomans. Strongest regional and historical fit, and well known abroad because of the Voyager record. | Recommended first choice. Traditional, so free to record ourselves. The theme is defiance more than leaving. |
| **Хубава си, моя горо** (Hubava si, moya goro, "You are beautiful, my forest") | A love song to the forest, written by someone leaving it. Fits a wide shot of the forest almost too well. | Not a folk song. It's a 19th-century composed song (words by Lyuben Karavelov, music by Emanuil Manolov), but both men died over a century ago, so it's public domain. It sounds more like a choir song than a Rhodope song. |
| **Притури се планината** (Prituri se planinata, "The mountain collapsed") | Famous slow song, very dramatic for an opening. | The well-known Stefka Sabotinova version is a copyrighted arrangement. Which region it comes from needs checking before we claim it as a Rhodope song. |
| **Заплакала е гората** (Zaplakala e gorata, "The forest wept") | A forest grieving for a fallen haiduk. Strong forest imagery. | Better for a death or the winter low point than for the village. Region needs checking. |
| A Rhodope gurbet/parting song | The game's own story: men leaving, women staying. | Ideal on theme, but needs digging in Stoin or the Miladinov collection to find one with a usable melody. Worth an afternoon of research if the stretch goal goes ahead. |

### "Generating it ourselves"

The safest way to produce it is a real singer: one Rhodope-style singer recorded remotely (with a kaba gaida drone, which can be sampled) is enough for the intro and doesn't cost much. AI-generated singing in this style tends to sound wrong in the ornaments and the open-throated tone, and the competition may have rules on AI-generated assets, so check those rules before relying on it.

## Sound effects and ambience needed

### Ambience beds (per season, plus day and night)
- Beech forest, fir forest, pine ridge, open clearing, gorge with stream, ruins, village.
- Wind through leaves (summer full canopy), dry leaves rattling and falling (autumn), wind through bare branches and snow (winter), dripping and meltwater (spring).
- Weather: light rain, heavy rain on canopy, thunder in the mountains, snowfall hush, blizzard.
- Water: streams, a river crossing, a waterfall, rain on a tarp.

### Wildlife
- Birds by season and time: blackbird, woodpecker, cuckoo, owl at night, ravens in winter.
- Wolves: distant howls (the warning), close growls, attack, pack movement.
- Bears: huffing, crashing through undergrowth, roar, attack.
- Small animals: deer bolting, boar in undergrowth, insects in summer nights.

### The men
- Footsteps on leaf litter, moss, rock, mud, snow, ice, water.
- Movement through undergrowth and ferns (important: this is how danger and cover sound).
- Chopping, felling a tree (the creak and crash), splitting wood, hammering, building a cart.
- Gathering: berries, mushrooms, digging, carrying heavy loads (breathing, strain).
- Fire: striking flint, kindling catching, a small fire, a big fire, embers, stamping it out.
- Cooking, eating, sleeping (breathing, snoring), coughs and shivers when cold.
- Cart wheels on forest paths, loading and unloading.

### Weapons and combat
- Flintlock rifle and pistol: cock, prime, shot (close and echoing across valleys), reload. First pass made 2026-11-12: `sound/flintlock.py`
  (the lock and pan, the bang in four kinds of place, the far shot, the reload's sounds), played by
  `src/smyana/gunfire.lobster`.
- Hammers and axes as weapons, knife.
- Ottoman soldiers: horses, distant voices in Turkish, their gunfire, a horn or drum signal.

### UI and game state
- Soft, wooden or natural UI sounds (no synthetic bleeps): selection, order given, pause.
- Cache hidden, cache found (and the sound of finding a cache already raided).
- Knowledge learned at the ruins, need warnings (hunger, cold, tired), a man dying.

## Voices

Dominic also mentioned voice acting. Proposal:
- **In-game barks in Bulgarian** (with subtitles), short: acknowledgements, warnings ("Вълци!"), complaints about cold and hunger, names called out. Twenty men can share four or five voice actors.
- **Narration for the cinematics**: either a Bulgarian narrator with subtitles, or English narration over Bulgarian song. A Bulgarian voice is more authentic and fits the competition theme.
- **Sung voices**: the men singing at the fire and the women in the village are music and voice at once, and are a strong reason to record with real Bulgarian singers.
- Ottomans speak Turkish (or Ottoman Turkish), short lines only.

## Sourcing options

From cheapest to most authentic. A mix is likely.

1. **Sample libraries, composed in-house.** Fastest and cheapest.
   - Strezov Sampling *Rhodope 2* (Bulgarian choir) and its *Balkan Ethnic Orchestra* (kaval, gaida, gadulka, tambura, tapan), recorded in Bulgaria with real folk musicians.
   - LibreWave *The Shepherd's Flute* (kaval and related flutes).
   - Any decent orchestral library for strings and low brass.
   - Risk: sampled folk voice and gaida can sound stiff on exposed solos.
2. **Hire a composer plus remote soloists.** A composer writes with samples, then a real kaval player, a kaba gaida player and one or two Rhodope singers replace the exposed parts. Bulgarian folk musicians do remote session work, and the cost is small compared to an orchestra. This is the recommended route for the competition demo.
3. **Record a choir and a small ensemble in Sofia.** Sofia has session orchestras and choirs that record game and film scores. More expensive, but it would give the opening and homecoming the "Outcast" scale. Could be a later step if the demo grows.
4. **Traditional melodies.** Folk melodies like "Izlel e Delyo Haydutin" are traditional and can be freely re-arranged and re-recorded, but existing recordings (Balkanska, archive recordings) are copyrighted and need licensing. Using our own recordings of traditional songs avoids that.
5. **Sound effects.** Freesound.org (check each licence; CC0 and CC-BY are fine with credit), commercial packs (e.g. Sonniss GDC bundles, free each year), and our own field recordings. Ideally, a trip to record real Rhodope forest ambience would give the game an authentic base layer and good archive material.
6. **Archives for research, not for use.** The Bulgarian National Radio folk archive and the Institute of Ethnology and Folklore Studies (Bulgarian Academy of Sciences) hold large collections of Rhodope recordings, useful for finding the right songs and texts.
