# The style of Rhodope music: research notes

Status: research by Claude, 2026-11-10, asked for by Dominic. Read from the web in one sitting,
not from recordings or from a musician. Where a point rests on one source, or on Claude's own
general knowledge, it says so. A Rhodope musician should check all of it before we lean on it.

## What makes it sound like the Rhodopes

| Trait | What the sources say | What it means for our music |
|---|---|---|
| Five-note scale | The old style of the Smolyan area is pentatonic: five notes with no half steps between them (BNR). Scholars link the region's drone singing to these "anhemitonic" melodies (Kaufman, abstract only). Other parts of the Rhodopes use ordinary modes too (Plovdiv lecture). | Write tunes on five notes, for example E G A B D. This is the single quickest way to sound right, and it is why the tunes feel open and old rather than sad in a Western way. |
| Slow and unmeasured | Most songs are slow. Many, like the working-bee (sedyanka) songs, have no meter at all, yet keep "a distinct inner pulse" (Plovdiv lecture). The rhythm is "slow and languid" (BNR). | No bar lines. Write in seconds and breaths, not beats. Long held notes, then short falls. |
| Simple meter when there is one | Dance tunes are mostly plain 2/4 (the pravo horo), unlike the uneven 7/8, 9/8 and 11/8 of the rest of Bulgaria. A few exceptions exist. | Even our "fast" music should not borrow Thracian or Shope rhythms without care. The uneven-meter chase cue in the music plan is less Rhodope than we thought: see "What this changes" below. |
| Wide range, gentle line | Songs often span more than an octave; women sing high, men are baritones and basses. Melodies "move softly", with ornaments "woven into the melody without disrupting its flow" (BNR, Plovdiv lecture). | Big, calm shapes. Ornaments are small flicks inside a smooth line, never fireworks. |
| The voice copies the gaida | Vocal ornaments resemble the bagpipe's, and a particular sliding between notes (glissando) is a mark of the style (Plovdiv lecture). | Our kaval and voice lines should slide into notes and use the same flicks as the pipe. |
| One voice, mostly | Rhodope singing is mainly a single line: a soloist sings, the others repeat together. Men and women sometimes sing in unison, which is rare elsewhere (Levy). | A lone voice, or twenty men in rough unison at the fire, is the true sound. Thick choir harmony is a 20th-century concert sound, not a village one. |
| Three pockets of two-part singing | Around Velingrad, in Nedelino, and around Zlatograd there is two-voice singing with a held drone and close, clashing intervals. Nedelino's is on UNESCO's list as "an island in the monophonic sea of Rhodope songs". | Keep this rare and special. It suits the Roman ruins or the homecoming, not everyday travel. |
| The gaida plays the songs | The kaba gaida's repertoire is mostly instrumental versions of songs. It usually sounds through the whole song when it accompanies a singer. At weddings it plays slow free-rhythm songs in procession (Levy, Plovdiv lecture). | Gaida and voice share one tune. We can hand a melody from one to the other. |
| Plain, older playing | Older players ornamented sparingly, kept calm tempos and stayed close to the sung version. Fast, showy playing is a recent habit. Chromatic notes were almost unknown before the mid 1900s (Knowledge journal article). | For the 1800s, less is more: no virtuoso runs, no chromatic notes. |
| What they sing about | Working-bee songs (the largest group), shepherd songs, wedding songs, laments, heroic and historical songs, and songs of men going away to work (gurbet). The region has almost no calendar songs such as carols or harvest songs (BNR, Plovdiv lecture). | Our subject, men leaving for work, is one of the region's own song types. |

## The kaba gaida

- A very large, low bagpipe. The melody pipe (chanter) is long with a curved end; the bag is
  lamb, kid or sheep skin. The high "dzhura" gaida of other regions is not used here.
- **Pitch:** the drone is most often E (Wikipedia). The chanter's home note is the E above
  middle C, with the drone sounding low beneath it (accordion tutorial; the "two octaves below"
  is Claude's understanding of gaidas in general, not confirmed for the kaba).
- **Range:** about a ninth: a fifth below the home note and a fifth above it. Plain scale notes
  only, except the third, which can be major or minor (accordion tutorial, one source).
- **Tuning is loose.** It was never standardised like the Thracian gaida, because it was kept
  out of state ensembles. Each pipe is a little different (Levy). Slightly "off" tuning is right.
- **Ornaments.** A bagpipe cannot stop its sound or play louder and softer, so the player
  shapes the tune with flicks of the fingers:
  - the **"bip"**: a quick touch of the fifth above the home note (the thumb hole) at the start
    of a note. Rhodope playing has a near-constant patter of these;
  - **mordents** (note, neighbour, note), which give Rhodope tunes a "rounder feel";
  - a **rolling ornament**: mordents in a row with a bip between them.
  (All from the accordion tutorial, which studies the player Petar Yanev. One source.)
- **Massed gaidi.** Many kaba gaidi playing together (the "100 kaba gaidi" ensemble, and
  gatherings of hundreds of pipers) is a modern but much-loved sound of the region.

## Other instruments

- **Kaval:** wooden end-blown flute in three parts, with a large range. Plays alone or alongside
  the gaida.
- **Tambura:** long-necked lute, the main instrument for accompanying songs, especially among
  the Bulgarian Muslims (Pomaks) of the Rhodopes.
- **Tapan** (big drum) and **chan** (bells; sheep and goat bells are a sound of the mountains).

## What this changes in our plan

1. **Write the main theme on five notes**, unmeasured, with a range over an octave.
2. **The men sing in unison**, not in harmony. Two-part drone singing is kept for one or two
   special moments.
3. **The chase cue** in `music-and-sound.md` was planned in a "driving uneven meter". That is
   Thracian or Shope, not Rhodope. A hard, fast 2/4 with tapan and gaida would be truer to the
   place. The Ottomans could have their own sound instead. Proposal, for Dominic to decide.
4. **Bells** (chan) are worth adding to the sound list: a flock heard far off says "people near".
5. **Tuning should not be too clean.** Sampled or synthesised pipes want a little drift.
