# Surviving the journey: needs, food, fire, loads

*Claude's design, 2026-11-12, written before building, for Dominic to change. Everything here is a **Proposal**
until he agrees it. It is phase 3 of the world plan (`world.md`, issue #50 and its sub-issues #65 to #73), and
leans on what is already agreed: fire feeds the men and draws danger (D-009); needs, crafts, undergrowth and the
homecoming (D-010); the UI is extremely minimal, if not none (D-021); carrying is people (the design doc); no dull
jobs at work sites (D-008).*

## The idea in one breath

Every night is a choice between eating and being found (D-009). Everything in this phase feeds that one choice. The
men need food, warmth and rest; food comes from what they carry and what the wood gives in its season; the fire is the
only way to cook and the best way to stay warm, and its light can be seen. What they carry slows them, and what they
carry home is the point of going. None of it is shown with a meter. You read it off the men and the world.

## The needs (#65)

Each man has three needs, each from 100 (fine) down to 0 (spent). They are the man's, not the band's.

| Need | Falls | Comes back |
|---|---|---|
| **Fed** | About 60 a day; faster when he works (felling, digging, wading) or carries a full load | A hot meal at a fire fills it. A cold meal gives half |
| **Warm** | Toward the air's warmth: the season, the height of the place, night, wind, rain and snow, wading a ford | Near a lit fire it climbs fast. Sleeping in a camp, men close together, slowly |
| **Rested** | About 5 an hour awake, more walking loaded | Sleep in a camp at night, about 12 an hour |

- **What a low need costs.** Below 30, a need takes up to a third off his pace (each need its own share, so a cold,
  hungry, tired man is very slow). At 0 his health starts to drop: cold fastest (a winter night in the open with no
  fire can kill a man), hunger slower, tiredness only slows him and makes him miss more.
- **They come with him.** Kept on the man between places, like his health and his load.
- **The men eat once a day**, at the first camp after dusk, one portion of food each from what they carry. If
  some have none, the others share theirs out (a band, not twenty purses).

## Reading a man without a meter (#73)

- **Cold:** his breath shows as little white clouds in cold air (always in winter, at night in autumn), and a cold man
  stands hunched and shivers when he stops. The colder he is, the more often it shows.
- **Hungry and tired:** he is slower, so he falls behind the others on the trail. A tired man sits down whenever he
  stops.
- **Asleep:** he lies by the fire, or in the open in a cold camp.
- **The one panel left (Proposal):** with one man chosen, his name and, under it in a few plain words, how he is
  ("cold and hungry", "worn out", "well"). Later, who waits for him at home. Nothing for a group.
- Barks ("I can't feel my hands") come with the voices (the frame, #51).

## Camp, the fire and the night (#68)

- **Making camp** is one order: with men chosen, press **C**. They gather round the spot where they stand and lay a
  fire. A fire costs two bundles of wood off their backs; with none carried it still gets made, but takes about a
  minute longer while they hunt for dead wood (and makes a little noise).
- **The fire burning:** it cooks (the day's meal is hot and fills them), it warms everyone within four tiles, and
  wolves will not come within six tiles of it.
- **Its light and smoke are seen.** At night a fire is seen across the whole of a place by anything with a line to it
  (a patrol three times as far as it sees a man, through cover); by day its smoke is seen about twenty tiles off. A
  patrol that sees it comes to look, as it does for a shot. The bear smells food at a camp from fifteen tiles.
- **Putting it out:** press **C** again with the camp chosen. A cold camp is hidden, but the meal is cold (half), and
  late in the year cold camps cost warmth, and then health.
- **Sleeping:** men in a camp at night lie down and sleep, and get their rest back. They wake at first light, or when
  a shot is fired or something comes into the camp.
- **Walking on through the night** is just not making camp: the clock runs on, the men get no rest, and they are
  slow the next day.
- Night is about a minute of real time already (#13), so camp is a beat, not a chore.

## Food and gathering (#66)

- **Food comes in portions,** one a man a day. They leave the village with five days' food each.
- **The wood gives food in its season.** What there is to find is set per place and season in data (berries and
  herbs in late summer, beechnuts, mushrooms and wild pears in autumn, almost nothing in winter, sorrel and nettles in
  spring). It shows in the world: a patch of mushrooms at a tree's foot, a scatter of fallen nuts, a fruiting bush.
  Right-click a patch with men chosen and they gather it into their packs. What is taken stays taken on the way home.
- **Game.** Roe deer in the valleys and chamois on the ridge, shot with the rifles: a deer is several days' meat and a
  hide to take home. A shot is a shot, though, and it is heard (D-085). (Built after the patches.)
- **Goods to take home** are found the same way: herbs, hides, wild honey. They feed nobody; they are what the
  families need.

## Loads and weight (#67)

- **Each man has a pack** of about twelve: a portion of food is one, a hide two, a bundle of wood one (the bundle is
  carried on top, as now).
- **Weight is pace.** A full pack takes a fifth off a man's pace, wading or in snow more. A band walks as fast as its
  slowest man, so one overloaded man slows everyone.
- **A man lost is his pack lost,** unless the others go back for it: his pack lies where he fell.
- **Dropping the packs to run** (press **X**): the men drop everything where they stand and walk at full pace; the
  packs stay there until they come back for them, and anything can find them.
- The pack shows on the man: bigger as it fills, a hide rolled on top, the wood bundle as now.

## Caches (#69)

- With men chosen, right-click the foot of an old tree with **Ctrl**: they leave what they carry there, beyond a day's
  food. On the way home they pick it up by right-clicking the tree.
- **How well it is hidden** is the cover over it (#9): good under summer beeches, worse as the leaves fall, plain at
  the foot of a felled tree. A cache in sight can be taken by a patrol that passes; one with food in it can be dug up
  by the bear in spring.
- You read how hidden it is from the leaves over it, as you read cover now. No meter.

## Knowledge at the ruins (#70)

- One piece for the demo: **the Roman drain.** At the ruins, men right-clicked onto the drain study it. It takes about
  a game day for four men, quicker with more, and those men are not gathering or keeping watch.
- Those who learned it carry it home. If they all die, it is lost.
- It shows in the ending as drains in the village fields (D-011).

## Carts (#71)

**Proposed: cut from the demo.** The pass and the landslide already make a cart a burden, the horses and mules are
there as camp props, and the loads above already make weight matter. If there is time at the end, a cart built from
cut wood at the village edge on the way out is the shape it would take.

## The tally at home (#72)

Kept as the run goes: who came back and who didn't (by name, and where each fell), what they carried home, what they
learned. Shown by the homecoming and the ending (the frame, #51), not a score screen.

## Order of building

1. **The needs, camp, fire and night** (#65, #68, the reading of #73): the beat every day turns on.
2. **Food patches by place and season** (#66), then game with the rifle.
3. **Packs, weight and dropping them** (#67), then **caches** (#69).
4. **The drain at the ruins** (#70) and **the tally** (#72).

Numbers live in `data/survival.json`, so they can be tuned after playing.
