# Gathering

*2026-11-13. Built from the gathering proposal Dominic agreed that evening ("go ahead"); his words are in
`archive/notes/2026-11-13-dominic-off-the-path.md`. Decisions D-134 to D-137.*

Dominic, playing: "gameplay is moving my mouse over everything to find something". Gathering is now something you
see in the wood and hear from the men, not something you read under the mouse.

## Two kinds of thing

- **Food for the road:** ceps, wild pears, beechnuts, berries, rose hips, nettles, meat. A portion a man a day.
- **Things for home:** herbs and mountain tea, the wax of wild bees' honey, hides from the hunt, wool from another
  band's cache. They feed nobody; the tally at home names each kind (data/survival.json `wares`).

Both share the twelve places in a man's pack, so every pick is a choice.

## Stands

A place has a few **stands** of what its wood gives in the season (data/survival.json `forage`): two to five, not
dozens of patches. Each holds a good amount (ceps 10 to 16, wild pears 12 to 20) and is drawn bigger than life and
spread over a few tiles of ground, so it is seen from a way off.

- Light-loving finds (pears, the dog rose, berries, herbs) grow at the side paths' clearings and in open glades.
- Shade finds (ceps) grow deep under the old trees, eight tiles or more from the trail.
- High finds (mountain tea) grow on open stony ground.
- They keep away from the gates, the trail, the huts and the caches, and from each other.

The first man to come within eight tiles of a stand by day **calls it out**, once, over his head: "Ceps. Look.",
"Pears, there.", "Rose hips, still on the bush.". Right-click the stand with men chosen and up to four of them go
and gather it, one into their packs every four seconds between them. What is taken stays taken, on the way home too.
Sources for each find are in the `_doc` of data/survival.json `forage`.

## No words under the mouse

The cursor turns into a small drawn picture over something a right-click will use: a basket to gather, a hand to take
a pile or a cache, a knife for a carcass, a sight for game, a fire for a hut, a book at the ruins, a spade at a
landslide. Over anything else it stays plain. Only a man's name shows when you point at him.

## No felling

There was no reason to fell trees. Making camp sends two men a few tiles out for dead wood while the fire is laid;
carrying a bundle on from the last camp makes it quicker. Axes only clear what is in the way (a tree down across the
way, a bridge).

## The order signs and the bread

With men chosen, three small pictures sit under their feet: a fire (make camp, light it, put it out), a pack (put the
packs down, shown only when they carry something) and an open hand (halt). Beside them is a row of loaves, one for
each day the bread will last; the last is grey crumbs. The keys still work.
