# Smyana play-test 12: first-time player, 110 steps, 29 minutes of game clock

I reached the third place (the deep fir woods) on the fifth dawn with all 20 men alive, and nothing ever threatened them. Pictures are in `/tmp/smyana-playtest-12/NNNN.png`; the harness is stopped.

One caution on my own evidence: the pictures from steps 1-89 dropped out of my view during the session and I re-opened only fifteen of them for this report. For the others I rely on the log and on what I did at the time, and I say so where it matters.

## 2. The village at the start

**What it looks like.** A cluster of about ten two-storey houses: grey rubble-stone ground floors, white plastered upper floors with dark timber framing and rows of small windows, grey stone-slab roofs, white chimneys, and a wooden bay hung on the front of most (pictures 0013, 0020, 0025). There are cobbled lanes, low stone walls with timber tops, a stone trough or fountain, two women in black and red, and a brown horse on a meadow with terraces beyond. The art style is handsome and the houses read as Rhodope-type.

**Does it read as a real old mountain village? Only half.**
- The houses are the same model repeated, all facing the same way and about the same size. From the zoomed-out view (0025) it looks stamped.
- I saw no rooms built over streets. The lanes are open gaps between detached boxes, and no upper storey bridges a lane. If those exist they are in the part I could not reach or see.
- It is on almost flat ground. There are no stepped streets, no tall retaining walls under the houses, and no outbuildings, barns, woodpiles or church.
- The two women and the horse never moved in any picture between 0:07 and 1:30. I saw no dogs, hens, sheep or children. A faint smoke streak over one chimney (0013) was the only sign of life.
- A tan figure stands frozen with arms raised at the end of the lane (0013 at about 290,440; 0020 at 410,390). I could not tell whether it is a villager, a statue or a scarecrow.

**Broken, floating or clipping.**
- Two flat dark-green bars lie on the meadow beside the horse, like shadows of things that are not there (0013 at 880,400 and 1230,200; also 0020, 0023, 0025).
- The terrace banks east of the village are a jagged smeared strip with stretched pixels at the lip (0013, 0023).
- The cobbles end in a hard staircase edge against the grass (0003, 0017).
- In the lane the men stand packed against a house corner, some overlapping the wall (0020 around 520,130; 0025 around 600,270).
- Most of the village cannot be entered. After one lane the men refuse with "No way up there from here." The game's first place invites a look and then turns you away, with nothing to do there: nobody to talk to, nothing to pick up.
- One man was left behind at the bottom left for the whole village visit (0017, 0020, 0025 near 300,650) because my drag missed him. Nothing shows a count of selected men or that someone is left behind.

## 3. Did I know what to do next?

**Mostly yes for direction.** The "The way on" ring and the men's lines ("The lane runs east", "The track goes on across this place") always gave a heading. The pointer changing shape is a good idea.

**Where I was lost or confused.**
- "The way on" is not the exit. Three times the men stood on the ring and nothing happened (steps 61, 93, and day 2). You must guess to keep walking past it to the edge of the map. In the first place I did not understand this for about four minutes of game clock.
- The first place then changed at dawn by itself, so I still did not learn the rule.
- Finding carcasses. They are invisible in the undergrowth. I found the first only by scrubbing the pointer, and never found the second.
- Making camp. The flame pointer over a hut says "camp here", but right-clicking it only walks the men over. This cost me a whole night at the second hut.
- I never saw the letter screens between places. The harness passes through them, so I cannot judge them.

**Where I was bored.**
- Nights. Once the fire is lit there is nothing to do for 60-90 seconds of clock, with no way to hurry it and no events. Five nights is a long time to watch a dark screen.
- The walks are long with little on them, and the fir woods look the same everywhere.

**Reasons to leave the path.** Yes, and this is the best part: the men call out strawberries, deer, caches, huts and ceps, with markers. But the reward is nearly invisible. A loaf icon sometimes goes up by one, and two finds (the cache at 2:55 and the "go and look" at 13:22) gave me no line about what I got.

## 4. Survival

- **Food.** The loaves icon and the packs panel ("Bread for 4 days", picture 0067) are clear. I understood that hunting and gathering add to it. The bread went 4, 4, 3, 2, 1, then 2 after the ceps. Nothing said how much a deer or a patch is worth, so I could not weigh a detour against the time.
- **Hunting.** The idea (come up slow, right-click with the sight) got across. What "slow" means in a game with one walking speed did not. The first volley missed and the game blamed me for coming "too fast"; I have no idea how to approach slowly. The men also kept firing and reloading on their own for 20-plus seconds, which felt out of my hands.
- **Gathering.** Clear and satisfying the first time.
- **Tiredness.** One line on day 1 ("The men are tired, and hungry with it"). I saw no meter and no effect. On night 3 they had about an hour's rest, on night 5 almost none, and nothing happened.
- **Cold.** Never mentioned in 29 minutes, into late autumn with rain.
- **Camp.** The fire icon works. The zaptiye refusal ("Or ask again, and we light it") is a nice touch. The hut right-click does not make camp.
- **Danger.** None reached me. Wolves were announced on night 1 and in the fir woods, and I saw one at the screen edge (0104 top right), but nothing attacked, nobody was hurt, and the zaptiye are furniture. The survival game has no teeth for its first half hour.
- **Not explained.** What the hand icon does; what herbs, wax, hides and wool are for (the packs list them as "No ... yet"); "Right click an old tree to hide the packs at its foot" (why would I?); how many days the journey is; what the season change costs me.

## 6. How far, and did I want to go on?

Three places of an unknown number (village edge, beech valley, deep fir woods), to the fifth dawn, late autumn, 20 of 20 men alive, two loaves of bread.

I wanted to go on mildly: the look, the men's voices and the off-track finds are good, and I was curious about winter and the way home. But after 29 minutes nothing had gone wrong or been at stake, the nights dragged, and I had spent many minutes fighting the exit marker, the camp click and invisible carcasses. A real player would likely have quit in the first place when the men stood on "The way on" and nothing happened.

## 7. The five things to fix first

1. **Make "The way on" work.** Reaching the ring, or right-clicking it, should take the men out of the place, or the ring should sit where the exit is. Stop the place changing at dawn unasked.
2. **Make camp one obvious action.** A right-click with the flame pointer on a hut or on ground should make camp. As it is, the pointer lies and the men stand all night. Let the night pass quickly once the fire is lit, or put something in it.
3. **Give the first half hour a threat and a cost.** Tiredness, cold, the wolves and the zaptiye are all talk. Show the men's state, and let a missed night or a careless fire hurt.
4. **Show what you killed and what you got.** Mark carcasses, and say what each cache, patch and "go and look" gave. Explain "come up slow" in a game with one speed.
5. **Finish the village.** Vary and turn the houses, add the rooms over the lanes, let the men walk its streets, make the people and animals move, and remove the floating shadow bars and smeared terrace edges. After that, thin the fir-wood trunks and shadows and change the red man-shaped saplings.
