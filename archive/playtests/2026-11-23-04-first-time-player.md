# Playtest report 4: first-time player, 22:15 of game clock, 118 steps

Played by an agent on 2026-11-23 (evening) on the build with the forest's and the men's sounds, the music turning with the light, the hut and gathering fixes (PR 125, before D-150's faster night), from pictures and ordinary mouse and keyboard input only. The step budget ran out before the planned 30 minutes. The game never crashed.

## 1 What I think it is and when I would have quit

- I think it is a slow walking game. Twenty men with long rifles leave a mountain village in summer and must walk "out over the ridge, and home by spring" (Angel says this at 0:13).
- You point them along a lane through forest, feed them, and camp at night.
- I never learned what is over the ridge, why they go, or what counts as winning.
- In 22 minutes nothing threatened me. No animal, no stranger, no hunger effect, no man lost.
- The place name stayed "The village edge" for the whole session, and the season stayed summer.
- I would have quit at about 9-10 minutes. By then the bread was shown as "the last", berries had not visibly worked, and the second day looked exactly like the first.
- If I had stayed, I would have quit for certain at 15:06-16:55, when most of my men would not move for nearly two minutes.

## 2 The first five minutes, minute by minute

- **0:00-1:00.** Menu, Start. Todor: "Which way? The lane runs east, down to the beeches." I dragged a box over the men; rings appeared under them and three small icons appeared below them. The pointer became "an arrow with a ring (a right click sends the men here)". I right-clicked east and heard an "ack". The men walked. Arrow keys move the view; the view does not follow the men.
- **1:00-2:00.** I saw a bush with red berries and right-clicked it. The men walked to it and stood; nothing visible happened. Rain came up at 1:07. Hovering the icons gave names: a fire ("Make camp"), a sack (packs), a hand ("Halt"), and three small loaves. I clicked the sack at 1:31 and heard "pack_down".
- **2:00-3:00.** The pointer over the bush showed "a hand (pick these up)". I right-clicked again: an ack, the men bunched at the bush, and I still could not tell whether anything was picked. Rain faded at 2:03. The same tune started again at 2:10.
- **3:00-4:00.** Second bush, same result. It was already 14h-16h on the clock. The music ended at 3:53. At 3:54 Atanas said "The light is going. We should make camp." and dusk music began. At 3:58 Dimitar said "No way through there." It did not say where "there" was.
- **4:00-5:00.** I clicked Make camp at 4:24. Crickets at 4:25, fire lit at 4:36, crackling at 4:37, the men's humming song at 4:52. The screen went nearly black. This was the nicest moment of the session.

## 3 Controls found / never understood

**Found**
- Drag a box to choose men.
- Right-click to send them; the pointer text helps.
- Arrow keys scroll the view.
- Space pauses and shows "Paused" (found by accident at about 10:00).
- Clicking the little ring marker at the screen edge jumps the view to the men. This was very useful.
- The fire icon makes camp and later becomes a put-out-fire icon.
- The sack icon sets packs down.
- The hand icon halts.
- A left click on one man shows his name and "well" at the bottom left.
- Right-clicking a pack on the ground picks it up.

**Never understood**
- What gathering does. I right-clicked berry bushes at 1:04, 1:51, 2:24, 2:57, 3:27, 8:20, 12:49, 13:20 and 21:00. No "gather" sound was ever logged, no number or icon changed for the better, and no man said anything except "Strawberries!" once at 8:00.
- What the loaves mean. They went 3, then "That is the last of the bread" at 8:03, yet the picture still showed 3 loaves at 10:30, 2 at 12:45 and 1 at 19:30. The words and the icons disagree.
- What the packs are for and why I would set them down.
- Whether the men sleep or eat at camp. They stand in the same pose all night.
- Whether double-click chooses all men. It chose one.
- How to choose everyone with one key.
- Where the ridge is. There is no map, compass or arrow.

## 5 Pace

**The clock**
- A day and night together took about 6 minutes 15 seconds of play. Dawns came at 6:17, 12:41 and 19:05.
- Daylight ran about 4:20: one game hour per 20-30 seconds.
- Night at camp ran about 1:45: three game hours per 30 seconds. That speed felt right.
- The day feels short. "The light is going" came at 3:54, 10:18 and 16:42, each time after about four minutes of walking.

**Progress**
- I had three camps and was on my fourth day at 22 minutes.
- It was still "The village edge", still summer, still 20 men.
- I never saw a beech wood that was named, nor the ridge.
- With days this short and men this slow, I felt I was getting nowhere.

## 6 Sound and music (with clock times)

I could not hear anything; this is from the log only.

**Music**
- `24_menu` was never logged at the menu. `01_summer_day` began at 0:02 while the menu was still up.
- `01_summer_day` started at 0:02, 2:10, 6:17, 8:24, 10:00, 12:41, 14:48, 19:05 and 21:12. That is nine times in 22 minutes, and it was the only daytime track I ever heard.

- At 10:00 it restarted while I un-paused, so I am unsure of that one's cause.

- The only watery thing the log shows near one minute is "rain on the leaves, coming up" at 1:07 and fading at 2:03. No rain was visible in the pictures at that time, as far as I could see.

**Forest beds**

**3. Music at sensible moments**

- Dawn: `01_summer_day` at 6:17, 12:41 and 19:05. That is sensible.
- Repeats: the day tune nine times, the dusk tune three, the fire song three. The day tune is the problem. It plays back to back all day.

**4. New sounds**

**5. Pace**

- In 22 minutes and three days I never left "The village edge", and the season never changed.

**6. Gathering and scatter**
- Gathering did not scatter the men. They went to the bush as one clump and stood by it, or two men went and the rest stayed (12:49, picture 0074).
- With packs down (8:03-8:49) they went to the bush and stood in three neat columns (0052), still together.
- Nobody wandered off, so there was nothing to come back from.
- I could never tell that any berry was picked.

## 9 Ten changes, most important first

1. Fix men who ack and do not move (15:06-16:55). If they cannot go, have them say why, as they do with "No way through there".
2. Make gathering visible and audible: hands moving, a sound, a line of words, a count going up, the bush emptied.
3. Tell me where I am going: a spoken hint now and then, a mark at the screen edge for the lane or the ridge, and a place name that changes when I make progress.
4. Make night readable. Keep the men and the fire visible, and move the view to the camp when I make camp.
5. Make the bread honest. If "That is the last of the bread", the loaves should show it; and show what hunger does.
6. Give the day more than one tune, or leave quiet gaps. Nine plays of one two-minute track in 22 minutes is too many.
7. Longer daylight or faster walking. Four minutes of light barely covers two screens.
8. Let the view follow the chosen men, or give a key that does. I lost them four times.
9. When a gather or move order needs a long detour, say so, or show the path. At 21:00 they walked away from the bush with no reason given.
10. Explain the icons at the start with one line each, and give the hut, if there is one near, something that catches the eye.
