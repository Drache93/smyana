# Playtest report 2: first-time player, 37:40 of game clock, 127 steps

Played by an agent on 2026-11-22 from pictures and ordinary mouse and keyboard input only, on the build of PR 124. All times are the game's own clock (what a real player would have spent). The agent could not hear; sound comments come only from the log lines.

## 1. In one paragraph

I think this is a slow, wordless march game: you lead a band of 20 riflemen on foot through woodland, camp at a fire each night, and the seasons turn as you go (summer became autumn at 17:04). I guess the goal is to walk from area to area ("The village edge" then "The beech valley") and get the men somewhere, or home, alive before food and winter beat you. I am not sure of any of that: the game never told me a goal, in 37 minutes I saw exactly one line of words ("Strawberries!"), nothing ever threatened me, nobody died, and running out of food had no effect I could see. It is very pretty. I would have closed it at about 9:00 to 9:30 (stuck at a rock ledge, no goal, nothing had happened), and if I had pushed on, for good at about 34:40 when I lost all 20 men in the trees and could not find them. Confidence in "what the game is": low to medium. Confidence in "a new player will not work it out": high.

## 2. The first five minutes, minute by minute

- **0:00 Menu.** Title in Cyrillic, Start and Quit over a wood scene with men. Looked good. No music line in the log for the menu.
- **0:04 to 1:00.** Play starts: 20 men with rifles stand in a clearing. No text, no arrow, no hint. Music 01_summer_day starts at 0:05, which fits the bright morning. I waited 15 seconds; nothing. At about 0:20 I left-clicked a man: a name ("Spas") and three small pictures (fire, sack, hand) plus 4 loaves appeared. Hovering the pictures gives no label. At 0:32 a right click on the ground seemed to do nothing; at 0:37 a left click on the ground and Spas walked. I concluded, wrongly, that left click moves. At 0:45 a drag box selected many men (thin pale rings at their feet, easy to miss). WASD scrolls the view (guessed at 0:50).
- **1:00 to 2:00.** Clicked the fire picture at 0:55: the men walked together and a camp fire burned by 1:05. Rain began. First moment I was pleased. Then 45 seconds stuck (1:10 to 1:55): box-select then left click on the ground did nothing, twice, and dropped my selection. At 1:55 a right click moved the whole group. **So I first understood select-and-move at 1:55**, after believing the opposite for a minute.
- **2:00 to 3:00.** Marched right. The view does not follow the men. Saw a bush with red berries at 2:20, walked the men onto it, right-clicked it, clicked the sack picture: nothing visible from any of it.
- **3:00 to 4:20.** Scrolled one screen right, ordered a move into the wood; the men never came. Scrolled back to find them standing still. At about 4:20 I pressed space hoping to jump to my men. It paused the game with no sign on screen at all. I spent five orders thinking the game had frozen.
- **4:20 to 5:10.** Unpaused by pressing space again. Night fell very fast (19h at 4:30); I clicked fire at 4:45, the men found a clearing by themselves, lit a fire and lay down, one sitting watch. The best moment of the session.
- **Goal:** not understood in the first five minutes. Not understood at 37 minutes either. The first hint that there is anywhere to go came at 16:19 when the area changed.

## 3. Controls I found, and how

- Left click a man: select him (trial, 0:20). Drag a box: select several (trial, 0:45).
- Right click on ground: move the selection (trial and error, 1:55). Left click on ground: drops the selection.
- W A S D: scroll the view (guess, 0:50). About a screen a second, too fast to place.
- Fire picture: make camp; the men pick the spot, sleep till dawn. Changes to an "ashes" picture while camped; a move order breaks camp.
- Space: pause, with nothing on screen to say so (accident, 4:20).
- Escape: drops the selection. Tab: nothing I could see.
- **Never understood:** the sack picture (clicked at 2:35, nothing; it left the bar when food ran low around 19:00) and the hand picture (clicked at 6:35, 19:50, 27:50; maybe a halt or a slow walk; once the men started obeying again just after I clicked it, which may be chance).
- **Suspect exist, never found:** a way to pick strawberries or get food; a key to jump the view to my men; any use for the rifles; a map.

## 5. Pace

- **Nothing happens, at all, for 37 minutes** beyond weather, light and season. No animal, no stranger, no find, no danger, no choice. The only events are ones I caused (camp) and one area change.
- Nights: five of them, each 80 to 120 seconds of pure watching (5:10 to 6:30, 10:45 to 12:40, 16:50 to 18:05, 22:45 to 24:15, 29:15 to 30:50). The first was lovely. By the third I wanted a "sleep till dawn" skip. That is about 8 minutes of 37 spent waiting.
- The longest dead stretch was 6:30 to 16:19: nearly ten minutes of wandering one area with no aim.
- Days are short (about 4 minutes from dawn to dark), so I got roughly two and a half minutes of marching between camps. It feels like the day ends just as I get going.
- Too fast: nightfall (bright to nearly black in about 30 seconds) and the scroll speed. Nothing else came too fast. Being frozen between steps did not save me from anything, because nothing ever pressed me; a real player would only have been rushed at dusk, finding the men before the screen went black.

## 6. Sound and music as told to me

- **0:05 01_summer_day**: fits. Ran to 4:18, fine.
- **4:20 02_summer_dusk**: the light had gone golden at about 4:00. Good fit; the best-timed cue of the session.
- **6:06 19_the_fire**: the fire was lit at about 4:50. The fire song came 75 seconds late, at 4h, just before dawn, and then kept playing until 9:37 while I marched at midday. Wrong for three of its three and a half minutes.
- **9:39 and 13:53 01_summer_day** again. No dusk track and no fire song at all on night two (10:45 to 12:40). Inconsistent with night one.
- **16:19 to 16:26 leaving / arriving**: no music line for either screen. The one real "moment" in the game so far passed in silence.
- **16:28 02_summer_dusk** on arrival at 20h: fits the dark blue. Then no music line until 19:46, so either that fiddle ran over three minutes through the night, the season change and the autumn sunrise, or there was silence; the log does not say which.
- **17:04 autumn begins; 18:05 autumn dawn with red trees**: no cue. The biggest thing the game showed me had no sound. **19:48 03_autumn_day** came about 100 seconds after that dawn.
- **24:02 19_the_fire**: again at 3h, at the end of the night rather than when the fire was lit (22:45).
- **27:35 and 31:49 03_autumn_day**, three plays running in about 12 minutes. Through night five (29:15 to 30:50) the day track simply carried on. 04_autumn_dusk and 05_autumn_night never played in two autumn evenings.
- **36:03 19_the_fire** at about 20h when I had not lit a fire and could not see my men.
- **Sounds:** none in 37 minutes. No shot, howl or bear. With no wind, rain, fire, steps or water, the rain I saw at 1:05, 18:05 and 26:00, the stream at 13:30 and every camp fire were silent. That is the biggest hole: the picture is rich and the ears would get one slow tune.
- **Would a cue have told me something the picture did not?** No, never. Every cue followed the picture late or missed it. Cues I wanted: something when an order is refused, a voice when food runs out, a sting on the season turning, the fire song when the fire is lit, and a far-off howl at night to make the dark mean something.

## 7. What I enjoyed, and what made me want to stop

Enjoyed: the look, all of it. The first camp fire in the dark (5:10). Waking to autumn in the rain (18:05, 0086.png). Leaves colouring as I walked and the mist (20:30). Trees going see-through over the men. The men finding their own camp spot and one keeping watch. The names.

Wanted to stop: no goal. No words. Orders that silently fail (five separate stretches, about six minutes in total). Losing the men because the view does not follow them. The invisible pause. Three unlabelled pictures, two of which I never understood. Food that drains to nothing with no way shown to refill it and no result when it is gone. Nights that are the same wait each time. Nothing ever at stake.

## 8. Reason to leave the path? Hunger, cold, night, danger?

- **Leaving the path:** no reason given. I left it constantly, but only because I could not tell what the path was. Berry bushes are the one thing that looks like a reward off the path, and I could not use them. Angel said "Strawberries!" at 9:57; I missed it on screen and it changed nothing.
- **Hunger:** I worked out that loaves are food and drop about one a day (4 at the start, 3 at 6:35, 2 at 14:00, 1 at 19:00, half at 19:35, gone by 25:00). Then nothing happened for 12 minutes with no food and 20 men. So I do not understand hunger; from the screen it has no effect.
- **Cold:** not shown at all. I assume the fire is for cold. Nothing said so.
- **Night:** understood as "too dark to see, so camp". That worked without words, which is good. But night carries no threat.
- **Danger:** none seen. The men carry rifles that were never used.

## 9. The ten changes that would most help a first-time player

1. Tell me the goal in one line in the first 30 seconds, by a man's speech or a short letter: where we are going and why.
2. Show where a move order lands and say plainly, by sign or voice, when it is refused; today refused orders look like a broken game.
3. Make the view follow the selected men, or give a key that jumps to them, because losing them is what ended my game.
4. Teach select and right-click-to-move on screen in the first 20 seconds, and start with the whole band already selected.
5. Put the word "Paused" on screen when space is pressed.
6. Label the three pictures on hover (Camp, and whatever the sack and hand are), and make an on/off state obvious.
7. Make food readable and playable: let me click a berry bush to forage, and have men say they are hungry and visibly suffer when the loaves run out.
8. Put something in the first ten minutes: a howl at the first night, tracks, a stranger, an abandoned camp; anything that is not weather.
9. Point at the way out of each area (a worn road, a signpost, a man saying "east"), since I found the first exit by accident at 16:19.
10. Fix the music timing and add the missing sounds: fire song when the fire is lit, dusk and night tracks every evening, a cue for the season turning and for leaving and arriving, and fire, rain and stream sounds; also let me skip the night once the camp is made.
