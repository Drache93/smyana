## Summary

Shift is a strategy / role-playing survival game built on Creel, the Age of Anything engine, keeping its camera angle. A band of about 20 villagers in 1800s (or earlier) Bulgaria travels out from their village through forest and into the mountains, gathering resources, skills and knowledge, then has to bring them home before winter. The core tension is balance: carrying more slows you down, fewer survivors means less can be carried, and you are racing the seasons, bandits, bears and possibly the Ottomans.

The trees are the heart of the game, both as mechanics and as art. They are huge, you move beneath them, and they change through the seasons. They provide resources, cover and hiding places for caches, so as leaves fall (or you cut trees down) you lose cover and your caches become easier to find and steal. The art direction is soft, enchanting and open, defined by the colours of trees, leaves and ferns, meant to make you feel small.

### Mechanics mentioned
- Around 20 named villagers for the whole game. They look a little different but share the same abilities: build, chop, shoot.
- Survival matters because people are your carrying capacity.
- Too many resources slows you down. Carts can be built to move faster.
- Leave caches of food and resources on the way out and collect them on the return trip from winter, at the risk of them being found once the foliage drops.
- Learnable skills and knowledge to bring home (for example, a kind of drainage).
- Open question: a day and night cycle with sleep. A fire keeps people warm but draws people and animals to you; sleeping without one means people wake rested but hungry.
- Threats: the seasons, bandits, bears, possibly the Ottomans.

### Open questions raised in the memos
- How much the engine needs to change for this game, and whether it could be done as a plugin that extends it.
- Whether to include a day and night cycle and sleep.
