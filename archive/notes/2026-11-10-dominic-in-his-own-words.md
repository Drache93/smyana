**17:19**
> Let's make a public repo for it. It will be on my account, draft 93, and the repo, we're going to, same as with the other game, we're going to have an archive with all the pictures and information about all the work we've done and why we've done it. The very first entries of that will be the stuff from today. And I'll also get the other agent to add the changes that were done to Creel, to the game engine, to support all the lighting moods we need. So we'll include that as part of the work we're doing. Because we'll be obviously making the game, and we'll be making the updates in the engine in parallel to support anything the game is needing.

> Also, I think for simplicity, we'll go with the Latin name, Smeana. I think maybe on the game imagery or something, we can use the Bulgarian version as well, or just the Bulgarian version, but otherwise we use the, I think the English version for the repo name, and maybe the submission for the competition.

**17:22**
> the voice notes, the voice note transcript, the documents for the plan of the game, they all go into the repos markdown, and then we will also need to plan what the music will be like, whether we what kind of music we need for this, because we wanted to really have a feel of the Bulgarian stuff. It can't really be proper traditional Bulgarian music because it's too fast paced for this kind of game, but maybe I don't know enough about Bulgarian music, so we need to do some research into that. Also what kind of sounds we'll need, and the and if we sort of aim to start off with maybe like a 1 hour game, we can I think cover things quite nicely. What you've got so far with like the Roman ruins and the Ottomans chasing you through the pass, that's a really good start just from the design, and I think we can fill it in really well. We'll just need to redesign the village that you come back to, the cinematics at the start of the game or the end of the game, because I want the intro to be really really opening, like the game Outcast, where the opening scene has music playing from the Moscow Symphony Orchestra. We're not going to obviously go that far, but it's that same feeling of a wide opening that really introduces you to the forest and the wilderness of it, and your village. And then we'll obviously need some voice acting then as well.

**17:24**
> And because so much of the game will be AI driven, if not all of it, my notes and I think it's really important that my voice notes that I'm sending to you right now are all kept as part of the archive. It really shows the human touch. It's important that I think to show that even when a game or an art is created by AI, it's the human creativity that drives it all. That's really important, I think. And because none of the direction the game has, none of the feel, the mood would have come without the human touch. And I think it's really important that we show all that. Equally, the game engine Creo comes from experimenting. It comes from me wanting to make the game, the open source engine that I wanted as a child. And pushing that and being able to play a game that I've made with my own son is an amazing feeling. And being able to build on that, put it onto Steam Deck, put onto phone, it's just been really amazing just to see that what I can do with AI. Because a lot of people see it as a negative thing when you're using only AI, but when a person that's full-time employed can make a game and make something that really put their heart into something as a side project, that wouldn't have been possible without AI. It would have been months and months of work and learning, and this allows me to be creative and enjoy that creativity without needing to take up loads and loads of time as basically a part-time job in order to do it.
